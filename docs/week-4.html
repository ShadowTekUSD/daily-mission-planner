Hannah Survives a Benching Scandal, Sends Colby's 131 Points Home Empty-Handed

Team Name? 147.48, No 131.28: Hannah Wins the Shootout, and the Bench Argument

Good evening, and welcome to the game of the week. It was the week's lone upset, with Team Name? projected at 118.02 and No at 119.7. Hannah blew past her number by 29.46 points, and Colby blew past his by 11.58 and still lost. That's the fantasy equivalent of finishing a marathon in record time and getting disqualified for the shoes.

Hannah's engine was Kenneth Walker III at 30.9, plus Alvin Kamara with 22.8 and Brock Bowers with 20.6. Then came the real story. Puka Nacua put up 27.7 on her bench, along with T.J. Hockenson at 24.9. She started Jordan Addison, who gave her 9.1. She left 32.8 points on the pine and won anyway. Her best possible score was 180.28, which we assume Hannah is currently framing.

Colby had Tetairoa McMillan go for 45.2 against a 15.85 projection, the best single line in the league. Then the rest of the lineup went to lunch. Travis Kelce posted 3.5, David Montgomery 4.3 and Dontayvion Wicks 4.8. Meanwhile Deebo Samuel Sr. and waiver pickup Ollie Gordon II each scored 18.0 on the bench. Colby, you named the league "Lunch Money" and then handed yours over.

Hannah moves to 2-2 with a two-game win streak. Colby falls to 2-2 on a two-game skid. The bench decisions were both questionable, but only one team got to be questionable and victorious.

Top Play: Tetairoa McMillan, 45.2 points. A monster game on the losing side, and the league's highest individual score.

Not Top 10: Colby starting Travis Kelce (3.5) while 18.0 sat in two different bench spots. That's the kind of lineup you build in a dark room by feel.

Joe Benches 28 Points, Starts DJ Moore, Loses by 16.2. Detectives Called.

Pukachu, I Choose You 132.52, J-M-P-S JMPS, JMPS, JMPS! 116.32: Steve Stays Perfect, Joe Stays Creative

Pukachu is 4-0 and, by a margin of 0.17 points, scored almost exactly its projection of 132.69. That's the most boring-perfect Sunday imaginable. Steve's offense ran on Nico Collins (30.8 against a 15.3 projection) and Bijan Robinson (27.7), with Josh Allen adding 18.52 and George Kittle 17.0.

It wasn't flawless. Bucky Irving gave Steve 6.1 and the Steelers D/ST gave him 5.0. Christian Watson held the flex at 7.7 while Jaylen Waddle (14.5) watched from the bench. Steve left 10.6 on the pine, which would be a scandal if he hadn't won by 16.

Now to the crime scene. JMPS has an alleged lineup, and it benched Kyle Monangai, who scored 28.0, in favor of DJ Moore at 2.7. That's a 25.3-point swing in a game decided by 16.2. Add a Bills D/ST that gave Joe a single point and a kicker (Eddy Pineiro, 5.0) who is now unemployed, and you have a recipe for the 146.52 best-possible score Joe never reached. The good news is Jonathan Taylor (22.7), Sam LaPorta (22.4) and Brock Purdy (19.62) were solid, so this wasn't entirely Joe's fault. Mostly it was.

Steve is 4-0 on a four-game win streak and leads the league in points (603.16). Joe drops to 2-2 on a two-game skid and gets to keep refreshing the waiver wire, a hobby he is clearly passionate about.

Top Play: Nico Collins, 30.8 points, double his projection.

Not Top 10: Joe's decision to start DJ Moore (2.7) over Kyle Monangai (28.0). If the lineup were a crime, the bench would've called the cops.
