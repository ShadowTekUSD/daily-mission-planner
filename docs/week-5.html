John Pork Jr Wins With Two Goose Eggs in the Lineup, and "No" Is the Only Answer Colby Gets

Drake Maye drags John Pork Jr past "No" 113.76-109.04 as Colby's bench quietly holds the winning score

Good evening, Mission Planners. Danny Cordova's John Pork Jr is 1-4 and nearly out of the playoff conversation, yet it just won a game while starting De'Von Achane and Jadarian Price, who combined for 0.00 fantasy points. Nobody on the desk can explain it.

The explanation is Drake Maye. The QB put up 39.46 points against a 18.47 projection, which is more than the entire "No" backfield managed. Malik Nabers (29.10) and Garrett Wilson (23.40) joined him, and the three of them produced 91.96 of John Pork Jr's 113.76 points. The other six starters were along for the ride, and two of them didn't even buy a ticket.

Over on the other sideline, Colby Schnayer's "No" had a perfectly respectable day. James Cook III went for 22.80, C.J. Stroud added 20.94, and the Vikings D/ST dropped 13.00 on a 6.55 projection. That should have been enough. It wasn't, because Chase Brown (7.00 on a 17.44 projection) and Deebo Samuel Sr. (2.50 on 11.09) both clocked out early.

Now the turning point. Woody Marks sat on the "No" bench with 12.50 points while Brown started and scored 7.00. That 5.50-point swap would have put "No" at 114.54, enough to win by 0.78. The final margin was 4.72. Our investigators are calling it the most preventable loss of Week 5.

John Pork Jr did the same thing on a smaller scale. Wan'Dale Robinson (11.30) rode the pine while Price sat in the flex slot with a zero. John Pork Jr left 14.30 points on the bench and still survived, which is the fantasy equivalent of driving home with your eyes closed and arriving anyway.

The standings impact is lopsided. John Pork Jr snaps into a W1 streak, but at 1-4 and 11th with 8.9% playoff odds, it's still deep in the basement. "No" falls to 2-3 with a three-game skid (L3) and 33.45% playoff odds. That's seventh place and a league named after its owner, with a team named "No" that keeps getting the same answer.

Top Play: Drake Maye, 39.46 points on an 18.47 projection. He won this game almost single-handedly, and he was the highest-scoring player in the entire league this week.

Not Top 10: Starting Chase Brown (7.00) over Woody Marks (12.50) on a day "No" lost by 4.72. Marks would have been a 5.50-point upgrade, and "No" would have won by 0.78. Honorable mention to Deebo Samuel Sr., who gave "No" 2.50 points and a lot of questions.

Postgame Pressers:

"We started two guys who scored zero and still won. I'm not saying the process is good, I'm saying the process is undefeated this week." -A team source close to John Pork Jr

"We lost by 4.72 with 5.50 points sitting on the bench. Somebody check the bench for a smoking gun, because the evidence is right there." -A fan in the parking lot, outside "No" headquarters

Sweetheart Breaks Josh's Heart: Benched Studs Doom Scary Team

Don't Call Me Sweetheart outlasts Josh's Scary Team 146.6-133.78 as a kicker outscores a stable of stars

Welcome back to the highlight desk. Don't Call Me Sweetheart took down Josh's Scary Team by 12.82 points. The wild part is how many points the loser mailed to its own bench.

Lynn Stanley's squad beat its 115.5 projection by 31.1 points. George Pickens led the way with 28.0 from the WR slot, and Derrick Henry chipped in 21.0. Then came the plot twist: kicker Jason Myers dropped 23.0 points, outscoring Henry, Jameson Williams (18.7) and Christian McCaffrey (17.3). Somewhere, a running back is asking his agent for a kicker's workload.

Josh's Scary Team had a fine day by the numbers. Parker Washington went off for 30.4, Jordan Love added 25.28, and Kyren Williams contributed 19.6. The team beat its own projection by 16.85 and still lost, which takes real commitment.

The turning point arrived at WR, where CeeDee Lamb started and put up 2.9 points against a 19.13 projection. Mike Evans sat on the bench and scored 26.3. That's a 23.4-point swing in a game decided by 12.82. Jayden Daniels also sat there with 32.26 points, though Jordan Love's 25.28 at QB softens that one. Josh's best possible score was 168.36, and 34.58 points stayed on the pine.

Lynn isn't clean here either. Pat Freiermuth scored 21.5 on the bench while Quinshon Judkins gave the flex slot 8.0, a 13.5-point gift to the opposition. Don't Call Me Sweetheart won with 13.5 points left on the bench, which is a flex.

Don't Call Me Sweetheart moves to 4-1 on a three-game win streak, sits third, and has an 84.85% playoff probability. Josh's Scary Team falls to 2-3 with a one-game losing streak and 48.125% playoff odds. Josh has scored 662.94 points this season, third-most in the league, and has two wins to show for it.

Top Play: Parker Washington, who exploded for 30.4 points against a 14.11 projection. That was the best score of any starter in this game, and it still wasn't enough, which tells you everything about the rest of that lineup.

Not Top 10: Starting CeeDee Lamb (2.9) over Mike Evans (26.3). Josh's front office left 23.4 points on the bench in a 12.82-point loss. Our investigators are calling it the "Evans Evasion," and nobody can explain it.

The Worst Team Wins, and Yes, We Checked the Name Twice

Tyler's Gibbs-Powered Squad Survives The McNasties' Self-Sabotage 111.32-95.4

Good evening, and welcome to a showcase of two teams that both missed their projections by about 24 points. "The worst team" took the win anyway, 111.32 to 95.4, and moved to 2-3 with a W1 streak. The name is still a bold claim, but the standings now put them at 9th.

Jahmyr Gibbs did the heavy lifting with 37.7 points against a 27.0 projection, roughly a third of the team's total. Trey McBride added 21.6 at tight end. After that the offense was Dak Prescott's 12.64, Terry McLaurin's 12.2 and a lot of shrugging. Jeremiyah Love turned a 14.37 projection into 2.2, and Michael Wilson turned 16.82 into 7.28. Gibbs carried this team the way a guy carries all the groceries in one trip.

Meanwhile, The McNasties staged a full-scale lineup investigation, and we have the evidence. Adam left 20.14 points on the bench, and the best possible score of 115.54 would have beaten Tyler's 111.32. Jacory Croskey-Merritt dropped 14.2 on the pine while Tony Pollard started and posted 5.2. That's a 9.0-point swing at one slot. Jared Goff also put up 28.94 on the bench, 4.34 more than Burrow's 24.6 in the QB slot.

The kicker situation deserves its own segment. Tyler Loop scored 14.0, making him the third-highest scorer on The McNasties, ahead of Bhayshul Tuten, Jakobi Meyers and Tee Higgins. When your kicker is your number three, the offense has a problem. Harold Fannin Jr. added 1.4 at tight end. Tee Higgins had 4.2 and Pollard had 5.2, which is not a flex-worthy resume.

Tyler wasn't perfect either. The Buccaneers D/ST would have scored 6.0 against the Jaguars' 4.0, so he left 3.4 points on the bench. It didn't matter, but we saw it. The win keeps his playoff odds at 46.8%.

For Adam, it's a 3-2 record, an L1 streak, and a 4th-place standing with 52.5% playoff odds. That is respectable, but this score was the lowest of the week in the entire league. The standings are friendlier than the scoreboard.

Top Play: Jahmyr Gibbs, 37.7 points on a 27.0 projection. He tied for the second-highest individual score of the week and outscored every starter on The McNasties by at least 13.1 points.

Not Top 10: The McNasties' bench management. Croskey-Merritt (14.2) sat while Pollard (5.2) started. Goff (28.94) sat while Burrow (24.6) started. The roster was good enough to win by 4 points, but the lineup lost by 15.92.

Postgame Pressers:

"We're 2-3 and we won with a team that missed its projection by 24 points. We'll take it. Please don't ask about the name." - a team source for The worst team, standing near the Gibbs jersey rack

"We had the points. They were just sitting on the bench, politely waiting to be asked." - a team source for The McNasties, staring at a depth chart in the parking lot

Jose's Supreme Team Survives Its Own Lineup, Hands JMPS a Third Straight Loss

Jose's Supreme Team 129.12, J-M-P-S JMPS, JMPS, JMPS! 111.56, as Joe Leaves a Whole 32.4-Point Tight End on the Bench

Good evening, and welcome to the upset of the week. Jose's Supreme Team entered as the projected underdog (115.84 to 118.18) and finished 13.28 points above its projection. J-M-P-S JMPS, JMPS, JMPS! finished 6.62 below its own. That's how you get a 17.56-point margin.

Matthew Stafford led the way with 27.32 points against an 18.18 projection. Davante Adams added 22.1, Aaron Jones Sr. chipped in 18.7, and Braelon Allen made it a 18.6-point party. Ka'imi Fairbairn the kicker dropped 17.0 on a projection of 8.99, which is how a kicker ends up in the highlight reel.

Jose did not exactly run a clean operation. The Rams D/ST put up -1.0, Amon-Ra St. Brown managed 12.9 on a 20.0 projection, and Rhamondre Stevenson sat on the bench with 17.5 while Tre Tucker gave Jose 7.5 in the flex. Kyle Pitts Sr. also watched 15.2 points from the pine. Jose left 19.2 points on the bench and still won by 17.56. Bold strategy.

Across the field, Spencer Shrader the kicker was JMPS's top scorer at 21.0. Sam LaPorta (19.8) and Drake London (18.8) held up their end. But Jonathan Taylor gave 11.2 on an 18.95 projection, Chris Olave managed 8.6 on 16.54, and the Cowboys D/ST posted -2.0.

Now the turning point. Isaiah Likely scored 32.4 points on Joe's bench. The guy in his flex, Malik Washington, scored 12.1. Do the math: swap them and JMPS wins 131.86 to 129.12. Joe also had the Broncos D/ST (14.0) sitting there while the Cowboys went -2.0. In all, 39.8 points stayed on the bench, which is more than twice the margin of defeat. Eleven moves on the season, and somehow not that one.

Both teams are now 2-3, but they're headed in opposite directions. Jose snaps into a W1 streak, sits 10th, and holds a 36.65% playoff shot. JMPS drops to L3, sits 6th, and has a 44.625% playoff shot, though that number is looking a little nervous.

Top Play: Matthew Stafford, 27.32 points. That's 9.14 over his projection, and it was the biggest single-game performance among starters in this matchup. Isaiah Likely scored more (32.4) but did it from the bench, so the committee does not count it.

Not Top 10: Joe benching Isaiah Likely (32.4) for Malik Washington (12.1) in the flex. That's a 20.3-point swing in a game decided by 17.56. Investigators are still checking whether Likely was waving a sign from the sideline. Honorable mention to the Cowboys D/ST for -2.0 while the Broncos D/ST (14.0) sat on the bench.

Postgame Pressers:

"We left 19.2 points on the bench and still won. That's called depth, and also luck," said a team source for Jose's Supreme Team, while a kicker reportedly took a victory lap in the parking lot.

"We're not saying the tight end on the bench cost us the game, but he scored 32.4 points and we lost by 17.56," said a team source for J-M-P-S JMPS, JMPS, JMPS!, staring at the lineup screen for the fourth straight hour.

Pukachu Evolves Into Final Form, Leaves The Backup PMD Fainted at 0-5

Steven's Squad Drops 162.4 on Darren's Winless Crew in a 54.94-Point Demolition, 162.4-107.46

Pack it up, folks. "Pukachu, I Choose You" put up a league-high 162.4 points in Week 5, and it was also the biggest blowout on the board. The projection said 128.7. Pukachu beat it by 33.7. That's not a win, that's a hostile takeover.

The offense came in waves. Christian Watson, sitting in the flex spot with a modest projection of 13.83, went off for 32.3 points to lead the team. Bucky Irving ran for 31.5, and Josh Allen added 30.5 at QB. Matthew Golden chipped in 20.3 for good measure. When your fourth-best starter scores 20, the opponent is just a spectator with a roster.

Now, let's be fair and needle the winners. Bijan Robinson managed only 15.8 against a 22.21 projection. Nico Collins posted 10.6, George Kittle 7.4, and the Steelers D/ST laid a 1.0-point egg. Tyler Bass, the kicker, scored 13.0 and outscored Collins and Kittle both. Pukachu still won by 55. Sometimes the football gods just hand you the trophy early.

Meanwhile, The Backup PMD came in projected for 116.73 and finished at 107.46, missing by 9.27. Justin Jefferson (18.8), Kyler Murray (18.26) and Emeka Egbuka (17.2) held up their end. The Texans D/ST dropped 15.0 and was a bright spot, but a defense can't carry a roster alone. Ashton Jeanty gave 10.7 against a projected 17.84, and Dalton Kincaid chipped in 3.7 at TE.

Here's the turning-point lineup decision. Hunter Henry sat on the Backup PMD bench and scored 13.4 while Kincaid took the TE slot for 3.7. Keon Coleman also did 7.7 on the pine while Carnell Tate gave 1.6 in a starting WR spot. That's 15.8 points left on the bench. Our fact-checkers did the math: even with a perfect lineup, the Backup PMD tops out at 123.26, still miles short of 162.4. Tidying the lineup would have made this a closer loss, not a win.

The standings tell the tale. Pukachu is 5-0 on a W5 streak, sits at No. 1, and carries a 96.9% playoff probability. The Backup PMD is 0-5 on an L5 streak, ranks 12th of 12, and holds a 6.5% shot at the postseason. One of these teams is planning January. The other is planning a very long bye.

Top Play: Christian Watson, WR, 32.3 points. He was projected for 13.83 and more than doubled it from the flex slot. Bucky Irving (31.5) and Josh Allen (30.5) were right behind, but Watson takes the crown.

Not Top 10: Starting Carnell Tate (1.6 points) at WR while Keon Coleman (7.7) sat on the bench, and Dalton Kincaid (3.7) at TE while Hunter Henry (13.4) rode pine. That's 15.8 points left on the bench, and the Backup PMD still wouldn't have won. It's an 0-5 start with a lineup-management problem on top.

Postgame Pressers:

"We've said it from the start: the other team can't stop what they can't see coming. Honestly, we just showed up, and the scoreboard did the rest," a Pukachu team source said, eyeing the 162.4 on the board.

"Look, we had Hunter Henry on the bench, we had Keon Coleman on the bench, and we had a lot of faith. The faith did not score 13.4 points," a Backup PMD team source said, staring at the 0-5 record.
